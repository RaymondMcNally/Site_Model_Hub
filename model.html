03 · Making it

From files to model

The same method as the full site model, at a size you can make yourself.

An egg-crate of true sections through your site: every piece is the ground cut through at one line of a grid.

A shallow relief engraved into a single sheet: quick, cheap and good to handle, though its heights are not to scale.

One print, no assembly. Book the printer: a 230 mm site at 0.2 mm layers is an overnight job.

Print the mould, then cast as many plaster models as you like from it.

You bring the file and the material; the technician sets up the toolpaths in VCarve Pro and runs the router.

  1. Base

    Cut a stiff base exactly your model size (for example 300 × 300 mm) from 5–6 mm board or MDF. Every layer lines up with its edges.

  2. Cut

    Run the blue engrave lines first as a light score, then the red cut lines. Test your settings on an offcut. Each piece is engraved with its layer number and level, e.g. 07 15 (layer 07, level 15 m).

  3. Stack

    Glue layer 01 to the base, square to the edges. Each next layer sits on the blue line engraved on the one below. With hollow layers, glue only on the strip the next layer covers.

  4. Draw

    Print the 1:100 plan at 100% on the sheet size it names. Use it as the base for your site analysis overlays and your Stage 2 design.

  1. Open

    Open the .3mf in Bambu Studio and choose the Bambu Lab P1S with a 0.4 mm nozzle. The model is already 230 × 230 mm and placed clear of the plate's front-left corner, which the P1S can't print on. Don't scale it, or it is no longer 1:200. If you move it, press Arrange (A) to put it back in a safe spot.

  2. Settings

    PLA in a light matt colour shows the contours best. Layer height 0.2 mm (0.12 mm for finer terraces), 2 walls, 10–15% gyroid infill, no supports. Brim is optional; the flat base sticks well on a textured PEI plate.

  3. Print

    Slice and check the time and filament before you send it. Steep squares take longer: the readout above gives the tallest point. Let the plate cool before you lift the print off.

  4. Draw

    The north arrow is recessed into the underside. Print the 1:100 plan at 100% on the sheet size it names and use it as the base for your site analysis overlays and your Stage 2 design.

  1. Cut

    Run the blue engrave lines first, then the red cut lines; test on an offcut. Every piece is engraved with its name and an arrow: A pieces run west to east (arrow points east, A1 is the most southerly), B pieces run south to north (arrow points north, B1 is the most westerly).

  2. Stand

    Cut a base board exactly your model size. Stand the B pieces upright on it, slots facing up, B1 on the west edge and every arrow pointing north.

  3. Slot

    Lower each A piece onto them, slots facing down, A1 at the south edge and arrows pointing east. Every slot is half the height at its crossing, so the tops meet flush. Ease the pieces in; if a slot is tight, sand it rather than force it.

  4. Read

    Each piece is a true site section at 1:200, spaced as the page says. Use them to draw your Stage 2 sections, and print the plan at 100% as the base for your overlays.

  1. Import

    Open the PNG in the laser software. It carries its resolution, so it should come in at your model size (for example 300 × 300 mm); check the size before you run it. North is the top of the image.

  2. Settings

    Use the software's 3D or greyscale engraving mode (in LightBurn: Image mode 3D Slice or Grayscale), where black engraves deepest. Depth comes from power, speed and passes: test on an offcut of the same material first.

  3. Material

    Birch plywood and MDF show the relief best; cast acrylic gives a frosted relief. Keep the extraction running: deep engraving makes a lot of smoke.

  4. Read

    The relief is a few millimetres deep, so it shows the shape of the ground, not true heights. Pair it with the 1:100 plan, printed at 100%, for levels.

  1. Material

    Prepare a block a little bigger than your carving (the setup sheet gives a size) and at least as thick as the model is tall. For true-scale heights that usually means laminated MDF or a block of high-density modelling foam. Make sure it is flat and square.

  2. VCarve

    Give the technician the ZIP. In VCarve Pro the STL is imported as a 3D model at its own size (it is already 1:200 in millimetres), with its top at the material surface. The setup sheet lists every setting.

  3. Toolpaths

    A 3D roughing pass clears the bulk, then a 3D finishing pass with a ball-nose bit gives the ground its surface. Toolpaths are saved with the post processor supplied with the router and loaded from its USB stick.

  4. Draw

    North is the back edge of the file (the top of the plan). Mark it on the carving before you lift it off the bed. Print the plan at 100% on the sheet size it names and use it as the base for your site analysis overlays and your Stage 2 design.

  1. Print

    Open the .3mf in Bambu Studio (Bambu Lab P1S, 0.4 mm nozzle). It is placed clear of the plate's front-left no-print corner; don't scale it. PLA, 3 walls, 15% infill, no supports. Use 0.12 mm layers if you can wait: every layer line shows in the plaster.

  2. Prepare

    Coat the inside with a release agent (a thin film of petroleum jelly or soft soap, or mould release spray), working it into the hollows with a soft brush. Stand the mould on a level board and mark north on its back wall: the side furthest from you as it sat on the plate.

  3. Pour

    Mix fine casting plaster by the bag's instructions; the page gives the volume to fill. Pour slowly into the deepest part, tap the sides to lift bubbles, and fill to the brim. Scrape a ruler across the rim: that flat face becomes the base of your model.

  4. Release

    Leave it until the plaster has set and cooled, then turn the mould over onto a cloth and ease the cast out: the walls are tapered so it lets go. Let the cast dry for a few days before you paint or draw on it. If a PLA mould warps from the plaster's heat, print the next one in PETG.

Levels come from the site's terrain model, which is usually bare earth: trees and buildings aren't in it. Traced features are approximate. Parts of a square that fall outside the survey are left at base level. The 3D print and mould follow the terrain grid every 0.4 m (2 mm on the print), so the ground is a fine faceted surface rather than stepped layers. CNC carvings over 600 mm use every second point (4 mm on the carving).
