What the model can and can't tell you

The shadows come from the terrain model: the banks, hollows and slopes inside the surveyed area.

Trees and buildings are probably not in it. Most terrain models are bare earth, with the canopy and structures removed. On site, much of the shade comes from them, so stand where you'd build and look.

Distant hills are not in it either. High ground outside the survey can hide a low sun altogether, in winter and early or late in the day. Sketch the skyline on site and note where the sun drops behind it.

Calculations use the site's position, —, and local clock time in Europe/Dublin, including summer time.

02 · Drawing it

Putting the sun on your site analysis plan

On a trace overlay of the 1:500 plan, record the sun the way architects do. Keep it simple, consistent and labelled.

  1. Sun arcs

    Draw three arcs around your site: 21 June, the equinox and 21 December. Mark sunrise and sunset bearings and the time on each. Use the Sun bearing readout above with the north point on your sheet.

  2. Shade

    Hatch the ground that's in shade at a few key times, such as 10:00, 13:00 and 16:00 on the day of your visit, and again in midwinter. Take the shadow from this page, then correct it on site for trees and the skyline.

  3. Aspect

    Mark the slopes that face south and catch the sun, and the slopes that face north and stay cold and damp. Outdoor rooms, entrances and places to sit want the warm, sheltered spots.

  4. Check on site

    Photograph the same spot at different times. Note where puddles, moss and frost sit; they show you where the sun doesn't reach.

03 · Wind and rain

The weather the site faces

Start with the region, then narrow it down to the valley. Your group dossier needs both.

The region

Wind. Ireland's prevailing wind blows from between south and west, off the Atlantic. Winds are strongest in the north and west, and gales are most frequent on northern coasts.

Rain. The west of Ireland gets about 1,000–1,400 mm of rain a year, and many mountain districts get more than 2,000 mm. The east is drier. Sites near high ground get the wetter end of the range.

Source: Met Éireann, Wind and Rainfall.

Gather it yourself

  • Find the nearest Met Éireann stations to your site on the historical data page and download monthly rainfall, temperature and wind.
  • Draw a wind rose: how often the wind comes from each direction, and how strong.
  • Draw monthly rainfall and temperature as a simple bar and line chart.
  • On site, note the wind on the day: direction, gusts, where it's still.
  • State the station name and distance from the site on every chart.

Make your own rainfall chart and wind rose

Download a station file from Met Éireann and drop it here. Everything happens in your browser; nothing is uploaded.

  1. Open Met Éireann's historical data page and choose the nearest station to your site. Synoptic stations have full hourly records, including wind; outside Ireland, use the national weather service.
  2. Download the hourly file for a wind rose and rainfall, or the monthly or daily file for rainfall only. Unzip it if needed: you want the .csv.
  3. Drop the CSV below. Pick the years (a 30-year span such as 1991–2020 is the standard climate average) and, for wind, the season.

No file? Fill in a template by hand from another source and drop it in: rainfall template (mm per month) · wind template (% of time from each of 16 directions). Always name the station, its distance from the site and the years on your chart.

04 · Microclimate

How the valley changes the weather

The weather on a sheltered site can be very different from the open ground a few kilometres away. These are things to look and feel for on the site visit.

ShelterTrees, banks, walls and buildings slow the wind. Where is it calm enough to sit?
FunnellingValleys, streets and gaps between buildings channel wind along their length. Does the wind follow them?
Cold airOn still nights cold air drains downhill and pools on low ground. Hollows and watercourses will be the frostiest places.
DampNorth-facing banks and deep shade stay wet. Look for moss, ferns and standing water.
Warm spotsOpen, south-facing ground catches the low sun. Use the Aspect map to find candidates, then check them on site.
SoundWater, wind, traffic and birds are part of the climate of a place. Where can you hear them, and where can you hear yourself?
